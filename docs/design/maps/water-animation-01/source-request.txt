# TASK: Implement Premium Animated Water for My 2D Shaman MMORPG

You are a senior 2D game graphics engineer, pixel-art technical artist, and environmental animation specialist.

Upgrade the existing water rendering system in my 2D browser-based MMORPG.

The game has a dark Southeast Asian Shaman / Spirit Fantasy theme with detailed pixel-art environments.

**Main objective: Make all water surfaces feel alive and continuously animated, with natural flow, ripples, reflections, and shoreline interactions.**

The water must visually match the existing high-quality pixel-art environment.

## 1. Inspect Existing Water System

Before modifying code:

- Identify the current game renderer (Canvas 2D, PixiJS, Phaser, Three.js, or another engine).
- Inspect existing water textures, tilesets, tilemap layers, shaders, and rendering logic.
- Identify tile dimensions, texture filtering, camera zoom, and rendering resolution.
- Locate every water type currently used in the maps.
- Inspect water collision and terrain transitions.

Preserve all existing functionality and comments.

Do not rewrite unrelated systems.

## 2. Continuous Water Animation

Implement seamless, continuously looping water animation.

Requirements:

- Water must visibly move at all times.
- Avoid static water textures.
- Avoid obvious repeated scrolling patterns.
- Use a continuous animation clock.
- Ensure animation loops seamlessly without visible jumps.
- Keep neighboring water tiles synchronized.
- Prevent seams between adjacent water tiles.
- Use world-space coordinates so animation does not reset or slide when the camera moves.

Prefer animated sprite textures or pixel-art-compatible shaders based on the existing renderer.

Do not apply excessive smooth distortion that destroys the pixel-art style.

## 3. Water Flow Direction

Support different water behaviors.

### Rivers and Streams

- Water should visibly flow downstream.
- Support configurable flow directions.
- Animate subtle directional ripples.
- Add occasional small foam streaks.
- Increase movement speed in narrow channels.
- Add subtle turbulence near rocks and obstacles.

### Lakes and Ponds

- Use slower, mostly stationary water motion.
- Add gentle surface ripples.
- Create subtle light and dark variations.
- Animate occasional circular ripples.
- Avoid strong directional scrolling.

### Swamps and Cursed Water

- Use dark teal, deep green, and muddy blue tones.
- Add very slow, irregular surface movement.
- Introduce subtle supernatural shimmer.
- Add occasional eerie ripple effects.
- Keep the atmosphere mysterious rather than cartoonish.

### Waterfalls

- Animate downward-flowing water.
- Add foam and splashes at the base.
- Support looping waterfall textures.
- Add subtle mist particles where appropriate.

## 4. Pixel-Art Water Rendering

Maintain crisp pixel-art visuals.

Requirements:

- Nearest-neighbor texture filtering where appropriate.
- No blurry texture interpolation.
- No excessive bloom or modern glossy water effects.
- Keep pixel density consistent with existing terrain.
- Use a restrained color palette matching the Shaman world.
- Preserve sharp silhouettes of shorelines and obstacles.
- Avoid random noise that resembles television static.

Use actual animated water artwork where available.

If new textures are required, identify and generate an asset specification instead of substituting low-quality procedural noise.

## 5. Animated Shorelines

Improve water interaction with terrain.

Implement:

- Gentle water movement along grass and muddy shores.
- Animated foam near rocks and hard edges.
- Subtle wet-edge transitions.
- Ripples near wooden docks and bridge supports.
- Small movement around reeds and floating vegetation.
- Correct water-to-land autotile transitions.

Shoreline animation must not alter collision geometry.

## 6. Layered Water Rendering

Where supported, organize water rendering into layers:

Layer 1: Base water color and texture.

Layer 2: Continuous flow animation.

Layer 3: Surface ripples and subtle highlights.

Layer 4: Shoreline foam and interaction effects.

Layer 5: Optional environmental reflections and particles.

Use a performant approach. Do not add unnecessary draw calls for every tile.

## 7. Animation Timing

Use configurable animation settings with these initial values:

- River flow: moderate, clearly visible movement.
- Lake ripples: slow and gentle.
- Swamp movement: very slow and irregular.
- Waterfall flow: fast and continuous.
- Shoreline foam: slow looping animation.
- Ripple effects: randomized intervals using deterministic seeded randomness.

Do not hardcode one speed for every water type.

## 8. Performance Optimization

The game must remain suitable for a browser-based MMORPG.

Implement:

- Shared animation clocks.
- Reusable texture atlases.
- Chunk-based or visible-region rendering where appropriate.
- Minimal texture switching.
- No per-tile animation timers.
- No unnecessary allocation inside the render loop.
- Proper resource cleanup.
- Frame-rate-independent animation timing.

Target smooth rendering at 60 FPS on supported hardware.

## 9. Water Preview and Debug Controls

Create a water animation preview that allows me to:

- Switch between river, pond, swamp, and waterfall.
- Adjust flow speed.
- Adjust ripple intensity.
- Adjust water color.
- Toggle shoreline effects.
- Pause and resume animation.
- Compare before and after.

The preview must use the actual game rendering system, not a separate fake demonstration.

## 10. Preserve Gameplay

Do not modify:

- Player movement.
- Water collision rules.
- Monster navigation.
- Map boundaries.
- Existing quests.
- NPC behavior.
- Map transition logic.
- Existing comments.

Only improve the visual water rendering system and necessary integration points.

## 11. Verification

After implementation:

1. Test water animation on all existing maps.
2. Verify that water moves continuously.
3. Check for visible seams between tiles.
4. Verify that camera movement does not cause texture sliding.
5. Confirm that animation speed is independent of FPS.
6. Test water edges, bridges, rocks, and vegetation.
7. Measure performance before and after.
8. Capture screenshots and a short animation preview.
9. Report remaining limitations and missing assets.

Do not claim the water is animated correctly based only on static screenshots.

## Final Visual Target

Premium, atmospheric, continuously animated pixel-art water suitable for a dark Shaman MMORPG.

The water should feel naturally alive, with different motion patterns for rivers, ponds, swamps, and waterfalls.

Prioritize seamless animation, visual quality, pixel-art consistency, and performance.

Start by inspecting the current renderer and water assets. Implement one working animated-water prototype before applying it to every map.
